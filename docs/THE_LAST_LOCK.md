# The Last Lock

Nov 4, 2026 · @Clement P Tom

## Premise and lore

A rogue and an old priest's dog cross a war-torn Hispania in 757 to recover a saint's relic split in three, and to re-seal the thing that King Roderic let out beneath Toledo.

**Logline:** The priest you smuggled wine for has vanished, leaving his dog, half a map, and a confession. Now you must find the relic he hid forty-four years ago, before the war feeding the thing under Toledo makes it whole.

### The relic: the Snow of Eulalia

Saint Eulalia of Mérida was a girl martyred around 304. Her legend says a white dove flew from her mouth as she died, and snow fell out of season to cover her body. Mérida's Basilica of Santa Eulalia was real, and her relics really were carried north during the conquest.

In the game, the reliquary holds the power of that snow: it **stills**. It freezes, quiets the dead, and seals what should stay shut. To hide it, the three clerics split it:

1. **The Bone** — a finger bone of the saint in a silver case. Hidden by Abbot Ervigio beneath Mérida.
2. **The Dove** — a silver dove that sings when evil is near. Hidden by Brother Gundemar in the Roman gold mines of Las Médulas.
3. **The Snow** — a crystal vial of meltwater that never warms. Hidden by Sisnando… in the collar of his dog. (Reveal, Act III.)

### The horror: the House of Hercules

There is a real medieval legend that Toledo held a sealed tower, and each Visigothic king added a lock to its door. Roderic broke them all, hoping for treasure. Inside he found a painted cloth of foreign horsemen, a prophecy of the kingdom's fall.

In the game, the cloth was a warning, not the treasure. Below it lay what Hercules buried in Hispania: **Geryon, the Three-Bodied**, the giant of the west he killed in his tenth labour. Geryon is not dead. It is hungry, and it eats slaughter.

Geryon does not care about cross or crescent. It feeds on every massacre, whoever does it. Its servants, the **Hollowed**, are people of every faith emptied out and refilled; they speak with three voices at once. At Guadalete it took its first new body: King Roderic. That is why his body was never found.

The Visigothic kings' locks were never the true seal. The last lock was always Eulalia's snow, kept at Mérida and carried to Toledo each generation. Now the wars of 756–757 are a feast, and Geryon is nearly whole.

## Characters and factions

Five core characters carry the story; the factions are deliberately grey, so the horror is Geryon and not any people.

| Character | Role | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Corvin of the Pass** | Player character | Smuggler and lockpick in the Great St Bernard pass, about 30. Cynical mouth, soft heart. Opens every lock he meets, so the game ends with him having to close one. His little brother Pio died on the pass when they were boys, and Corvin let go of his hand; the Hollowed learn to call to him in Pio's voice. |
| **Father Sisnando** | Mentor, the missing priest | Hispano-Visigoth, about 70. Keeps a stone shelter for travellers on the pass. Kind, funny, carrying a guilt he only half confesses. Until Act III the player should not be sure whether he is a victim or the villain. |
| **Barro** | Companion dog | Huge Alpine mountain dog, ancestor of the St Bernard. Sniffs out trails, growls at the Hollowed before they reveal themselves, drags Corvin out when he falls. Unknowingly carries the Snow. |
| **Hafsa bint Malik** | Ally, scholar | Physician and copyist from Córdoba. Her brother was Hollowed. Knows the Arabic tellings of the locked house of Toledo. Sharp, impatient, brave. |
| **Idris al-Masmudi** | Ally, guide | Berber veteran who deserted in the 740 revolt and stayed in the empty Duero lands. Quiet, superstitious, the best tracker alive. |
| **Brother Gundemar** | Antagonist | The third cleric of 713. Broke in captivity and found Geryon's voice in the dark. Now the Hollow Shepherd, gathering a flock for the feast. |
| **King Roderic** | Final boss vessel | The last Visigothic king, now Geryon's first body. |

### Factions

- **Emirate garrisons** — soldiers of a new and nervous emir. Suspicious of foreigners, not evil. Some help, some get in the way.
- **Asturian raiders** — warbands from the northern kingdom, burning the Duero valley to deny it to the emirate. Brave and brutal; their burnings feed Geryon too.
- **Frontier brigands** — deserters from every army. The human threat in the empty lands.
- **The Hollowed** — Geryon's flock. Christian, Muslim, Jewish and pagan alike; emptiness has no faith.

## Intro

The game opens on a playable flashback as young Sisnando in 713, then cuts forty-four years forward to Corvin in the Alps.

### Prologue — "The Siege Ends" (Mérida, 713, playable as Sisnando)

1. **Cold open.** Black screen. A girl's voice sings a Latin hymn. Snow falls on a burning city. Title card: *Emerita Augusta, Year of Our Lord 713.*
2. **The basilica.** Mérida has surrendered. Abbot Ervigio splits the reliquary on the altar and hands out the pieces. The player learns basic movement and interaction carrying the vial.
3. **The escape.** The three clerics slip out through the Roman aqueduct channels. First horror beat: the drowned dead in the water turn their heads to follow them. Stealth tutorial.
4. **The hiding.** In the hills, the trio separates to hide their pieces. Sisnando's own hiding is shown only as a closed door; the player never sees where. (Fair-play clue for the later reveal: he kneels beside a stray dog.) As Ervigio seals his niche and they walk away, something knocks three times from inside the stone. Nobody turns around.
5. **The capture.** A patrol takes them on the road west. The soldiers are tired men, not monsters: one gives Sisnando water. That night Gundemar is taken away separately and does not come back. Ervigio dies of fever.
6. **The ship.** Sisnando escapes at the coast and hides aboard a merchant ship. From the deck he watches villages burn along the shore; one fire looks like a three-headed figure rising. Fade to white.

### Act 0 — "The Hospice in the Snow" (Great St Bernard pass, 757)

1. **Corvin's run.** Corvin smuggles salt over the pass for his old boss, Malaspina. An avalanche buries him. Player wakes under snow, short struggle sequence, then a dog's face.
2. **The hermitage.** Barro has dragged Corvin to Father Sisnando's stone shelter. Sisnando patches him up and refuses payment except "come back and fix my roof."
3. **Seasons montage.** Short playable vignettes over a year: fixing the roof, a dice game Corvin cheats at and Sisnando lets him win (Corvin mentions Pio once, then changes the subject), rescuing a pilgrim with Barro. This is the tutorial for combat, lockpicking and dog commands.
4. **The confession.** A winter night by the fire. Sisnando tells the story of Mérida and the relic, and says the war in Hispania has woken "the one we kept asleep."
5. **Vanished.** Next morning: door open, fire cold, Sisnando gone. In the snow are tracks of three people walking in perfect step. One set is Sisnando's own boots: unhurried, no drag marks. He seems to have walked out willingly. Barro is whining in the corner. On the table: a letter, half a map, and the silver collar Sisnando always polished.
6. **Malaspina.** Corvin's old boss turns up to collect a debt and claim the shelter. First mini-boss fight. Corvin leaves for Hispania with Barro.

## Story progression

Four acts, each ending in a boss and a relic beat: Mérida (the Bone), the empty Duero lands (the trail), the Pyrenean cave monastery (the Dove and the Snow), Toledo (the seal).

| Act | Title | Locations | Goal | Boss | Payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | The Road of Ash | Siege lines at Narbonne, Pyrenees, Mérida | Recover the Bone | The Abbot Who Keeps Watch | First relic piece; meet Hafsa |
| II | The Empty Land | Duero valley, ruined villages, Las Médulas | Find the Dove; follow Gundemar's trail | The Gold-Eater | Dove is gone; learn where Sisnando is held; meet Idris |
| III | The Hollow Shepherd | Cave monastery under the cliff, Pyrenees | Rescue Sisnando | Brother Gundemar | Dove recovered; the Snow revealed in Barro's collar; Sisnando's confession |
| IV | The House of Hercules | Toledo and the caverns beneath | Re-seal Geryon | Geryon, wearing Roderic | Ending choice |

### Act I — The Road of Ash

Corvin crosses into Septimania and talks his way through the Frankish siege lines at Narbonne. Over the Pyrenees the land is scarred: villages burned by raiders of one side or the other. The half-map points to Mérida.

In Mérida, Corvin's lockpicking gets him into the sealed crypts beneath the ruined basilica, where he runs into Hafsa, who is there for the same reason: the Hollowed who took her brother came through here. Uneasy alliance. Ervigio's ghost still guards the Bone, but forty years of waiting have twisted him into a watcher who cannot tell friend from thief. After the fight, the Bone shows a vision: Gundemar, younger, carrying the Dove north-west.

### Act II — The Empty Land

The Duero valley is a deliberate wasteland: no lords, no harvest, villages standing empty with tables still set. This is the dread act — long quiet stretches, Barro growling at nothing, the Dread meter climbing. Idris, who has lived alone here for fifteen years, finds them before the Hollowed do.

At Las Médulas, the Roman gold mines, Gundemar's hiding place is open and empty. The Hollowed miners have dug themselves into the walls. Below lives the Gold-Eater. After it falls, Corvin finds Gundemar's scratched message and a recent trail: a flock heading east to the mountains, with an old priest among them.

### Act III — The Hollow Shepherd

A cave monastery built under an overhanging cliff, where Gundemar keeps his flock and Sisnando as a prisoner. Infiltration act: stealth, disguise among the Hollowed (Dread rises fast while disguised), freeing prisoners.

Gundemar wants the Snow, to destroy it. In the boss fight his hounds pin Barro, and snow bursts out of the collar: the vial was there all along. Gundemar falls, the Dove is recovered, Sisnando is freed. That night, Sisnando confesses the rest: he was in Toledo in 710. He held the lamp while Roderic broke the locks.

### Act IV — The House of Hercules

Toledo is an emirate city on edge, fresh from civil war. Corvin smuggles the party in. Beneath the city, the House of Hercules is a descending dungeon of broken locks. The core mechanic inverts: the rogue now has to re-close each lock with the relic pieces while Geryon's pull grows. At the bottom waits Roderic, and behind him, the rest of Geryon.

### Threads that run through every act

Three threads escalate across the acts: doubt about Sisnando, Pio's voice, and what the enemies are.

| Act | Doubt about Sisnando | Pio's voice | Enemies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | His letter says not to follow. His own tracks show he walked out willingly. | Corvin mentions Pio once and changes the subject. | Human only: smugglers, wolves. |
| I | Ervigio's ghost calls him "the lamp boy" and curses him. | In the aqueduct, a child laughs Pio's laugh. Nothing is there. | Mostly human: brigands, garrison patrols. The uncanny stays in crypts and at night. |
| II | The Hollowed ask Corvin, "Have you come from our brother Sisnando?" Gundemar's message calls him "little brother." | The walls of an empty village call, "Corvin, I'm cold." Barro refuses to move. | Possessed and changed people take over: Hollowed, Ash Walkers, Wall-Sleepers. |
| III | Corvin first glimpses Sisnando unchained, praying with the flock. (Payoff: he walked in willingly to try to save Gundemar's soul.) | A Hollowed child speaks to Corvin in Pio's voice while he's disguised. | Things that were never human: Carrion Hounds, the three-headed alpha. |
| IV | The confession is out; now the doubt is whether he'll hold. | Geryon uses Pio's voice, then Corvin's own. | Lock-Wardens and Geryon. Human, possessed and inhuman all mixed. |

## Storyboard and dialogue

Sixteen key scenes in play order. Each gives the shot, then the lines.

### Scene 1 — The splitting of the relic (Prologue)

*Shot:* Candlelit altar, siege drums outside. Ervigio's hands pry the reliquary apart. Snow drifts down inside the church.

**ERVIGIO:** One piece for each of us. Apart, they are only a bone, a bird and water. Together, they are the last lock.

**GUNDEMAR:** And if they take us?

**ERVIGIO:** Then you forget where you put it. Forget it so well that pain cannot find it.

**SISNANDO:** Father, the snow… it's falling indoors.

**ERVIGIO:** She knows. Go.

### Scene 2 — The ship (Prologue end)

*Shot:* Night sea. Young Sisnando at the rail. A line of fires along the coast. One fire rises taller than the rest and splits into three.

**SAILOR:** Don't look, priest. Whatever burns there isn't yours anymore.

**SISNANDO:** *(whispering)* It was always mine.

### Scene 3 — The dog's face (Act 0)

*Shot:* First-person under snow. Darkness, muffled heartbeat. Digging. Light breaks: a huge dog's muzzle, then a wet tongue.

**SISNANDO:** *(off-screen)* Barro, leave him some face, he'll need it for lying.

**CORVIN:** *(coughing)* Who says I lie?

**SISNANDO:** Your boots. Those are a bishop's boots, my son, and you are no bishop.

### Scene 4 — The confession by the fire (Act 0)

*Shot:* Low firelight. Barro asleep across both their feet. Wind outside. Sisnando turns the silver collar in his hands.

**CORVIN:** You never talk about Hispania.

**SISNANDO:** Because I left it burning. *(pause)* We had a relic. Saint Eulalia. A girl who died so young the sky covered her in snow out of pity. We split her in three and hid her from the war.

**CORVIN:** Worth much?

**SISNANDO:** *(laughs)* Everything. Not to a buyer. There is something under Toledo that eats wars, Corvin. Her snow kept it sleeping. And now the war has started again.

**CORVIN:** Old men's stories.

**SISNANDO:** Yes. I am an old man. And the story is coming up the mountain.

### Scene 5 — Vanished (Act 0)

*Shot:* Dawn. Door swinging. Three lines of footprints in the snow, perfectly in step, leading downhill. Barro won't follow them; he sits and howls.

**LETTER (Sisnando's voice-over):** *If you are reading this, I was too slow. Do not come after me. Go after her. The map shows Mérida. Keep Barro close; he knows more than either of us. And Corvin — fix the roof.*

### Scene 6 — Hafsa in the crypt (Act I)

*Shot:* Corvin picks the last lock of a crypt gate. A blade touches his neck.

**HAFSA:** That lock was sealed by the emir's governor. Which makes you a thief.

**CORVIN:** And you a woman alone in a Christian crypt at midnight. We could report each other.

**HAFSA:** The things that took my brother came out of this crypt. They spoke with three mouths. Do you know what they are?

**CORVIN:** No. But I know a man who did. And his dog doesn't like you.

*(Barro sniffs her, then sits at her feet.)*

**HAFSA:** Your dog has better manners than you.

### Scene 7 — The Abbot Who Keeps Watch (Act I boss intro)

*Shot:* An underground chapel where snow falls from the ceiling. A tall robed figure, face hidden, kneels over a silver case. Its back is full of keys driven into the flesh.

**ERVIGIO (ghost):** Sisnando? No. Another thief. Every year another thief.

**CORVIN:** I'm here for him. He sent me.

**ERVIGIO:** The lamp boy? The lamp boy sends no one. He only ever brings the dark. Forget… forget so well that pain cannot find it… *(screaming)* I forgot WHO I WAS WAITING FOR.

*After the fight, released:*

**ERVIGIO:** Tell the boy I kept it. Tell him I'm sorry I kept it so long.

### Scene 8 — The set tables (Act II)

*Shot:* An abandoned village. Every house has bread on the table, gone to stone. Bowls of soup with dust skins. No bodies.

**IDRIS:** *(stepping from the shadows)* Don't eat anything. Don't sit. Don't answer if your name is called.

**HAFSA:** Who are you?

**IDRIS:** I came here to fight a war fifteen years ago. The war left. I didn't. *(to Barro)* Good. You hear them too.

**CORVIN:** Hear what?

**IDRIS:** The people who live here. They're still in the walls. Waiting for supper.

### Scene 9 — Gundemar's message (Act II end)

*Shot:* After the Gold-Eater falls, an empty niche in the mine wall. Scratched in Latin, translated on screen.

**GUNDEMAR (carved, voice-over):** *I hid the dove here. They hurt me until I came back for it. In the dark something told me how much it hurts to be one person. It offered me three. Forgive me, Ervigio. Forgive me, little brother Sisnando. I am coming for you.*

**HAFSA:** He knew where the old man lived.

**CORVIN:** For forty years. He was waiting until the war made his master hungry enough.

### Scene 10 — Among the flock (Act III)

*Shot:* Corvin in grey Hollowed rags, walking in step with them through the cave monastery. They chant in three overlapping voices.

**HOLLOWED (three voices):** One body is lonely. Three bodies are a king. One body is lonely…

*(Barro, hidden outside, whines through the cave mouth. A Hollowed child turns its head toward the sound.)*

**HOLLOWED CHILD (three voices):** There's a dog. Dogs are lonely too.

### Scene 11 — Gundemar (Act III boss intro)

*Shot:* A cave chapel under the cliff. Sisnando chained to the altar. Gundemar, stooped and smiling, two other faces faintly pressing out of his neck and shoulder.

**GUNDEMAR:** Little brother. You got old. Look at me. I never had to.

**SISNANDO:** You broke, Gundemar. That's all. Men break.

**GUNDEMAR:** I was broken. Then I was mended into more. *(to Corvin)* You brought the bone and the bird to me. How kind. Where is the snow, Sisnando? Where did you hide our little snow?

**SISNANDO:** Somewhere you'll never think to look.

### Scene 12 — The collar (Act III climax)

*Shot:* Gundemar's hounds pin Barro. Corvin is down. A hound's jaws close on the silver collar — and it cracks. White light. Snow erupts outward, freezing the hounds mid-leap.

**CORVIN:** The collar… it was in the collar?

**SISNANDO:** *(weeping, laughing)* The only one of us nobody would ever torture.

**GUNDEMAR:** *(freezing)* So cold… it was so warm being three…

### Scene 13 — The rest of the confession (Act III end)

*Shot:* A campfire on a mountain ledge. Sisnando, bandaged, cannot look at anyone.

**SISNANDO:** I wasn't only at Mérida. I was a deacon in Toledo in 710. The king wanted the treasure of the locked house. I carried the lamp. I watched him break every lock, and I didn't stop him, because I wanted to see too.

**HAFSA:** Then you're the reason—

**SISNANDO:** No. The armies would have come anyway; kingdoms fall. But what came up with them, what ate both sides at Guadalete… that was the lamp in my hand.

**CORVIN:** Forty-seven years is a long time to carry a lamp.

**SISNANDO:** Then help me put it down.

### Scene 14 — Entering Toledo (Act IV)

*Shot:* The city on its hill above the river, lantern-lit. Soldiers at every gate.

**IDRIS:** The gates are watched. The walls are watched. The river is watched.

**CORVIN:** Everything is watched. Nobody watches the sewers. Nobody ever watches the sewers.

**HAFSA:** You've said that before.

**CORVIN:** And I was right before.

### Scene 15 — Roderic (final boss intro)

*Shot:* The bottom of the House of Hercules. A vast cavern, broken locks embedded in the walls like teeth. On a throne of fused bodies sits a king in a rusted crown, his face young and wrong.

**RODERIC:** Little deacon. You brought me a light again.

**SISNANDO:** I brought you snow.

**RODERIC:** I was a king. I opened one door. One door, and they took my kingdom, my name, my grave. Do you know what it is to be the man history blames? *(the two shadows behind him rise)* So I let something bigger wear me.

**CORVIN:** You're not a king. You're a coat.

**RODERIC / GERYON (three voices):** And you are a lock-breaker. Join us. There are so many locks left in the world.

**GERYON** *(suddenly in a small boy's voice)***:** Corvin? It's dark down here. Why did you let go of my hand?

**CORVIN:** …Pio?

**SISNANDO:** Don't answer it. It wears whoever you miss most.

### Scene 16 — The last lock (ending, good path)

*Shot:* Geryon frozen mid-roar. The three relic pieces float together over the final door. Sisnando steps inside the threshold.

**CORVIN:** No. Come out. I'll close it from this side.

**SISNANDO:** It needs someone inside to hold the lamp. I held it once to open. Let me hold it to close.

**CORVIN:** Who fixes my roof?

**SISNANDO:** *(smiling)* Your roof. Good. Then you're staying on the mountain. *(to Barro)* Take him home.

*As the door grinds shut, a whisper comes from inside, in Corvin's own voice:* Please. Don't leave me here. *Barro growls. Corvin doesn't turn around.*

*Cut to:* Toledo at dawn, August. Snow falls over the city. In the street, a Christian baker, a Muslim guard and a Jewish child look up together.

## Enemies and bosses

Eleven regular enemy types, seven mini-bosses and four bosses, escalating from human to possessed to never-human until, in Act IV, the player can no longer tell them apart. Every monstrous enemy comes from Geryon; human enemies come from every side of the war.

### Bosses

1. **The Abbot Who Keeps Watch** (end of Act I). Ervigio's ghost, keys driven through his back from every thief he caught. Phase 1: a snowstorm arena; he attacks whatever moves. Phase 2: he forgets where he is and the arena shifts back to 713 Mérida mid-fight. Phase 3: every candle dies. Blind in the dark, he hunts by sound: command Barro to stay silent, throw stones to lure him, strike when he turns. Defeat releases him rather than kills him. *Reward: the Bone.*
2. **The Gold-Eater** (end of Act II). Hollowed miners fused into a giant inside the Roman mine galleries, swallowing gold and spitting it molten. Fought by collapsing old Roman tunnels onto it, using the mines' real water-sluice method. *Reward: Gundemar's carved message, Sisnando's trail.*
3. **Brother Gundemar, the Hollow Shepherd** (end of Act III). Human-sized; two faces emerge from his body as the fight goes on. Phase 1: commands his flock. Phase 2: the faces split off as separate bodies, one speaking in Pio's voice. Phase 3 is scripted: the Snow bursts from Barro's collar. *Reward: the Dove and the Snow.*
4. **Geryon, the Three-Bodied** (finale). Phase 1, **Roderic**: a kingly duellist with a rusted sword and crown. Phase 2, **The Host**: the two shadow bodies rise, one made of Guadalete's dead from both armies, one made of fire; all three must be damaged together. Phase 3, **The Giant**: Geryon stands at full height while the party re-closes the last locks with the Bone, the Dove and the Snow; it speaks in Pio's voice, then Corvin's own, to make you stop.

## Endings and gameplay hooks

Three endings hinge on whether Corvin, a man who opens locks for a living, chooses to close one.

| Ending | How you get it | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Last Lock** (default good) | Finish the game without selling or using the relic for yourself. | Sisnando seals himself inside. Snow falls on Toledo in August. Epilogue: Corvin turns the stone shelter into a hospice on the pass; Barro's pups pull travellers from the snow. A nod to the real St Bernard hospice and its dogs. |

### Gameplay hooks

- **Barro the companion.** Commands: track, guard, fetch, attack. He growls at disguised Hollowed, finds hidden doors, and drags Corvin out when he's downed (once per fight). If Barro is badly hurt, Corvin's Dread spikes.
- **Dread meter.** Rises in the dark, near the Hollowed, while disguised, and when Barro is away. High Dread brings whispers, false enemies and shaking aim. Each relic piece lowers the ceiling.
- **Locks as the core theme.** Acts I–III: picking locks opens paths and secrets. Act IV inverts it: each broken lock in the House of Hercules must be re-closed under pressure, using the relic pieces as keys.
- **Relic powers.** The Bone: briefly shows the dead's last moments (clue system). The Dove: sings when Hollowed are near (early warning). The Snow: freezes enemies in place, but overuse pushes you toward the bad ending.
- **Faction reputation.** Emirate garrisons, Asturian raiders and frontier brigands each react to how Corvin treats them. High standing opens alternate routes; Hafsa and Idris comment on your choices.
